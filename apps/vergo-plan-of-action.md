# VERGO — Plan of Action

**Situation:** ~£500 cash. Film job starting (Mon/Wed/Fri, 30 hrs). Dog walking Tue/Thu 9–4, phasing out over 8 weeks. Weekends free. 50 worker applications, no clients yet.

---

## 1. The problem with your week

| | |
|---|---|
| Mon / Wed / Fri | Film — 10-hour days, effectively unreachable |
| Tue / Thu 9–4 | Dog walking |
| Sat / Sun | Free |

**You currently have zero weekday business hours.**

Venues, catering companies, production companies and agencies are all reachable 9–5, Monday to Friday. That's when bookings get discussed and suppliers get added to lists. Right now you can't reach any of them, and no amount of weekend effort substitutes — nobody's answering the phone at a catering company on a Sunday.

That makes phasing out the dog walking the single highest-value thing in this plan, and it should happen faster than two months.

**Do the maths on one day.** A dog walking day is maybe £70–100, so £300–400 a month. One repeat client booking 100 hours a month is £365 a month in margin, recurring, growing. One client makes the swap break even. Two makes it obvious.

**Sequence:** drop one day within 4 weeks, the second within 8. That gives you Tuesday and Thursday free from early October — right as the autumn season starts.

---

## 2. What each day is for

**Tue / Thu 9–4 (once free) — selling.** This is the money time and it's the only time you have. Outbound calls, venue visits, meeting caterers and production managers. Protect it ruthlessly; it does not become admin time.

**Sat / Sun — delivery and vetting.** Events run at weekends, so this is when you staff your own bookings. Be on site personally for the first ten — that's how you find out what actually breaks. Weekends are also when candidates are free, so run vetting interviews around bookings.

**Tue / Thu evenings — vetting and admin.** Video interviews, payroll, invoicing.

**Mon / Wed / Fri evenings — nothing.** After a 10-hour day on a unit base you will not build a business. Plan for this rather than discovering it in week three.

---

## 3. The thing that ends this

Film M/W/F, dogs T/Th, Vergo Sat/Sun is a seven-day week for ten months. Founders don't usually fail at month ten; they fall over at month four and the business stops with them.

**Protect one half-day a week.** Sunday morning, or Saturday if you're not working. Non-negotiable. It's the cheapest insurance in this plan.

## 5. Cash — solve before demand

At £500 you cannot fund the work you'd win. One four-staff, six-hour booking is ~£356 of wages, paid weekly, with the client paying weeks later. Three bookings in a fortnight and you physically can't pay people.

Fix it in the terms, not with money:

- **50% deposit on booking, balance on completion.** Standard in event services. Halves your exposure immediately
- **7-day payment terms** until you've built a buffer
- **Full payment upfront** for private clients — weddings and parties expect it
- **Prioritise agencies and caterers** — they invoice their own client and pay fast

**Your £500:** insurance £156, domain and hosting ~£60, payroll free. Leaves ~£280 — that's one booking's wages and it's your ability to trade. Don't spend it on marketing.

## 6. The calendar decides everything

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sept–mid Dec** | The season. Venues and caterers are stretched and will try a new supplier because their usual one is full |
| **Jan–Mar** | Dead. Nobody buys at any price |
| **Apr–Jul** | Weddings, summer events. Steady |
| **Aug** | Quiet |

**This is why the £5,000 timing matters.** Six months into the film lands you in January — the worst month of the year to spend it. Two options:

1. **Spend smaller and earlier.** Save £800/month from the film and you have ~£4,000 by December. Put it into the peak when conversion is highest
2. **Hold it to next September.** Same money, three or four times the return

What you don't do is spend it flat across January to March.

## 7. Between now and September, for nothing

Free channels aren't a warm-up for paid ones — for six months they're the whole plan, and they suit a relationship-led business better anyway.

1. **Agency overflow.** Three calls this month. Fastest paid booking available to you
2. **Your existing network.** Pub managers, kitchen managers, everyone whose number you've collected. Not a broadcast — individual calls
3. **The 200-list.** Cold but targeted, worked on Tue/Thu
4. **The film set.** Ten months next to production managers, line producers and caterers. The single best-quality lead source you'll ever have. Do the job exceptionally and let it work
5. **Google Business Profile and reviews.** Free, and reviews compound
