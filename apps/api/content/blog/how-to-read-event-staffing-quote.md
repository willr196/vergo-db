## In short

- An hourly rate on its own tells you very little, because minimum hours, overrun blocks, late-night uplifts and fees decide what the invoice actually says.
- At the 2026/27 legal floor, a worker on PAYE costs an employer between £14.24 and £16.81 an hour before the agency pays for insurance, payroll or cover.
- Some agencies add booking or admin fees on top of the hourly rate, so ask for every possible charge in writing before you compare quotes.
- Minimum hours exist so staff are not crossing London for a shift that barely covers the journey, which is why we set ours at {{MIN_HOURS_WORD}} hours.
- Ask who interviewed the person turning up and what they were asked, because the rate tells you what you pay and nothing about who arrives.

## Why do three quotes for the same event come out so different?

Because the hourly rate is only one line. Two quotes at the same rate can produce very different invoices once you add the minimum shift, how overruns are billed, what happens after midnight, and any fees that sit outside the rate.

The other reason is that not every quote is paying for the same thing. Some cover a worker on payroll with holiday pay, National Insurance and insurance built in. Some don't, and the difference shows up in the price.

The way to compare them is to turn every quote into the same question. For my date, my hours and my headcount, what will the invoice say? Ask each agency to answer that in writing. A good agency can do it in a line, and the answers are easier to compare than the rate cards.

## What is the lowest hourly rate a quote can be built on?

There is a floor, and it is set by law rather than by the market. This is what it costs an employer to put one person aged 21 or over on a shift at the National Living Wage, before anything else is paid for.

| Line | Per hour | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| National Living Wage | £12.71 | Aged 21 and over |
| Rolled-up holiday pay at 12.07 per cent | £1.53 | 5.6 weeks divided by 46.4 |
| Gross pay | £14.24 | The least a worker can be paid |
| Employer pension at 3 per cent | Up to £0.43 | Where auto-enrolment applies |
| Employer National Insurance at 15 per cent | £2.14 | Once the Employment Allowance is used up |
| **Cost to employ, lowest to highest** | **£14.24 to £16.81** | Before insurance, payroll or cover |

That is only the cost of the person. The agency still has to insure them, run the payroll and keep people in reserve for the night somebody doesn't arrive. Plenty of staff are paid above the floor too, so the real cost of a good team is higher than this.

So when a quote comes in close to that range, or under it, ask how. There can be honest answers. If the answer is that the staff are self-employed, it is worth reading our post on [PAYE and self-employed event staff](/blog/paye-vs-self-employed-event-staff) first, because it changes who carries the risk.

We have lost work on price to a client who didn't know what staff cost at current rates. The National Living Wage goes up every April, so a budget built from last year's quotes can be wrong before anyone has made a mistake. These figures are correct for 2026/27 and you should verify them on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates) before relying on them.

## Which fees should you ask about before you book?

Some agencies add booking or admin fees on top of the hourly rate. We have heard of them rather than seen them on a quote ourselves, so we can't tell you what they usually come to. That is exactly why the question is worth asking. A fee you haven't been told about is the one that turns the cheaper quote into the dearer one.

Ask for every charge that could appear on the invoice, in writing, before you book. Booking fees, admin fees, uniform charges, travel, and anything charged per booking rather than per hour. If the answer is that there are none, ask for that in writing too. Our [published rates and terms](/hire) set out everything that can appear on our invoice.

## Why do staffing agencies charge a minimum number of hours?

So that the shift is worth turning up for. Someone travelling across London to a venue for a two-hour shift spends a large part of what they earn, and a good part of their day, getting there and back. A minimum makes the work worth taking, which means the people who arrive are people who wanted the shift.

Ours is {{MIN_HOURS_WORD}} hours per person. If your event really is two hours long, the staff are still paid for {{MIN_HOURS_WORD}}, so it is worth planning setup and clear-down into that time rather than paying for extra hours on top.

## How much does an event that runs late add to the bill?

Three things decide it: the size of the overrun blocks, the late-night uplift, and the time the uplift starts. A team that stays twenty minutes longer than planned is billed very differently in half-hour blocks than in whole hours, and the difference grows with the size of the team.

So check all three before you compare. A lower hourly rate with whole-hour overruns and an early uplift can cost more than a higher rate on a night that runs late. Our overruns are billed in {{OVERRUN_MINUTES}}-minute blocks, with {{UPLIFT_PCT}} per cent added after midnight.

## Why should you ask who vetted the staff?

Because the rate tells you what you'll pay and nothing about who will turn up. Clients ask us about our hiring process and our vetting before they book, and whether the person coming has been interviewed. They are right to ask.

Every worker on our roster is interviewed by our founder before they work a shift. The interview goes through their previous experience and how it relates to the work they'll be doing, describes the tasks on the shift, and checks they have the current knowledge to do them. Right to work is checked and documented for everyone.

Whoever you book with, ask the same things. Has the person coming been interviewed, and by whom? What were they asked? Has their right to work been checked? And can you ask for someone you liked last time? {{FAVOURITES_PROMISE}}

## What this means if you're comparing quotes

Put each quote into the same shape: your date, your hours, your headcount, and the invoice total with every fee included. Check that the rate is above the legal cost of employing the person, and ask how if it isn't. Then ask who interviewed the staff. The quote that answers all of that clearly is usually the one to book.

## FAQ

### Why is one event staffing quote so much cheaper than another?

Usually because the quotes cover different things. Check whether holiday pay, National Insurance and insurance are inside the rate, and whether the staff are employed or self-employed. Then check the minimum hours, the overrun blocks and any fees, because those decide the invoice.

### What should an event staffing hourly rate include?

For a worker employed on PAYE, the rate should cover their pay, holiday pay, employer National Insurance, pension where it applies, and the agency's insurance. Our rate has no booking fee or uniform charge on top. Ask any agency what sits outside its rate before you compare.

### Why do event staff have a minimum number of hours?

So the shift is worth the journey. Staff crossing London for two hours of pay would spend much of it getting there and back. Ours is {{MIN_HOURS_WORD}} hours per person, with overruns billed in {{OVERRUN_MINUTES}}-minute blocks.

### What happens if a member of staff doesn't turn up?

It depends on the agency, so ask before you book and get the answer in writing. Ours is set out in our terms. {{NO_SHOW_PROMISE}}

### Can I hire an agency worker permanently?

Yes, but tell the agency first. Under [regulation 10 of the Conduct of Employment Agencies and Employment Businesses Regulations 2003](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/3319/regulation/10), an agency that charges a transfer fee has to offer an extended period of hire as an alternative.
