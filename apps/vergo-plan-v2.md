# VERGO — Operating Plan

**Model:** Employment business. Vergo employs the staff, invoices the client, runs PAYE.
**Charge rate:** £18.50/hr
**Wage:** £13.25/hr, all ages
**Cost:** £16.00/hr
**Margin:** £2.50/hr

---

## 1. The unit economics

| | Per hour |
|---|---|
| Wage | £13.25 |
| Holiday pay (12.07%, rolled up) | £1.60 |
| Employer NIC (15% above £96/wk) | £1.03 |
| Pension (3% qualifying earnings) | £0.00–0.28 |
| **True cost** | **£16.00** |
| **Charge** | **£18.50** |
| **Margin** | **£2.50** |

**Under-21s cost £14.85** — zero employer NIC up to £50,270. Pay them the same £13.25 and the exemption is your margin, worth an extra £1.03/hr. Weight recruitment accordingly.

**Employment Allowance (£10,500)** covers your employer NIC until roughly £70,000 of above-threshold earnings. While it lasts, everyone costs £14.85 and your margin is £3.65. Claim it through payroll software — HMRC doesn't apply it automatically.

## 4. What you sell instead of price

At £18.50 you're not the cheapest, so price isn't the pitch. These are, in order of how hard they are for a competitor to copy:

**Confirmation in 30 minutes or the booking's free.** Most agencies take hours. Nobody at scale can guarantee this. You can, right now, and it's what a venue with a 6pm problem actually wants.

**No-show guarantee — replacement on site within the hour or the shift isn't charged.** This answers the only fear the client has. Impossible at 5,000 hours a month, entirely doable at 500.

**The same faces.** Send the same people back to the same venue so they know the layout, the manager, the service style. Large agencies structurally can't do this. Clients feel it immediately.

**You, by name.** One person who knows the booking and picks up the phone. Not a portal, not a call centre.

**Full compliance.** PAYE, holiday pay, employers' and public liability, documented right-to-work. Sounds like admin, but it's what productions, hotel groups and corporates check before they'll use you at all — and it's exactly what the cheap operators can't produce. Price doesn't open that door; this does.

**Above-minimum pay.** £13.25 against a £12.71 legal minimum, published on the site. Signals better staff and lower churn, and it's decisive for any client with a Living Wage policy.

## 5. Setup checklist

| Item | Status | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| PAYE scheme registered with HMRC | ~5 working days | Free |
| Payroll software with RTI | BrightPay/Moneysoft handle variable hours | £0–25/mth |
| Employment Allowance claimed | Through payroll software | — |
| Rolled-up holiday pay, itemised on payslip | Permitted for irregular-hours workers since Apr 2024 | — |
| Auto-enrolment assessment + declaration to TPR | Duty applies even where casuals fall below the trigger | — |
| Employers' liability insurance | **Legally required.** £5m min, £2,500/day penalty without it | — |
| Public liability | Not legally required, commercially essential — venues ask for certificates | — |
| Written statement of particulars, day one | Casual/zero-hours template | ~£1,000 one-off |
| Right-to-work checks, documented | £60,000 penalty per worker for repeat breaches | — |
| Client terms of business | Covers transfer fees, payment terms, cancellation | included above |

**Check the £13/month policy actually covers employees**, not just you as a sole operator. Cheap combined policies often don't, and you find out at the worst moment.

## 6. Cashflow — the thing that kills agencies

You pay staff weekly. Venues pay at 30 days, some at 45 or 60. You fund the gap.

| Hrs/month | Working capital needed |
|---|---|
| 400 | ~£3,500 |
| 800 | ~£7,000 |
| 1,500 | ~£13,000 |

At £2.50/hr you cannot fund growth out of margin — the cash goes out months before it comes back. Three defences:

1. **14-day payment terms** in your client terms from day one, not 30
2. **Payment upfront** for new clients until they've established a record
3. **Invoice finance** in reserve — 1–3% of invoice value, advances 80–90% immediately

Profitable agencies go under this way. It is the single most likely cause of failure in this model.

## 7. VAT

Compulsory registration at **£90,000 turnover** — that's total invoiced, not margin. At £18.50 you cross it at **405 hours a month**.

So: almost immediately. Business clients don't care (they reclaim it). Private individuals do — £18.50 becomes £22.20 to a wedding client. Factor that into which segments you chase.

## 8. The film contract

**Not part of this plan.** A one-off, personal arrangement outside the company: Will introduces crew, the production engages and pays them directly, and Will is engaged personally at £21/hr, 30 hrs/week (~£25,200 over the run). Vergo has no revenue, no cost and no employment liability from it.

**The one thing that carries over:** because Vergo employs nobody on this contract, the £10,500 Employment Allowance stays fully intact for Vergo's own trading. That covers employer NIC up to roughly 1,000 hours a month, so the working margin is £3.65/hr rather than £2.50 across the whole ramp-up.

## 11. What kills this

**Cashflow, not profitability.** See section 6. Most likely cause of failure.

**Winning work at £18.50 then discounting to hold it.** Trade clients will push. Every 50p you give back is 20% of your margin.

**The film contract eating the Employment Allowance.** Repricing it to £18.50 fixes this. Leaving it at £15.50 costs you £1.03/hr across everything else you trade.

**Volume without systems.** At 800 hours a month across 40 casuals, weekly payroll by hand is unmanageable. £25/month for proper software is not the place to economise.

**No-shows at thin margin.** A 24-hour booking makes £60. One failure and emergency cover wipes out several. The guarantee in section 4 is a sales asset — make sure you can actually honour it.
